# The 5 Performance Domains of AI in PPPM

Source: PMI, *The Standard for Artificial Intelligence in Portfolio, Program, and Project Management* (June 2026), Chapter 3. The performance domains are the *activities* side of the standard — where the 8 principles (see `principles.md`) are the values/lens, the 5 performance domains are what practitioners actually do. They are interrelated, interdependent, and run concurrently — not sequential phases.

## 1. Managing Stakeholder Expectations About AI
Change management, clear communication, and training programs tailored to specific stakeholder groups, so people understand, accept, and can contribute to an AI initiative's success rather than just tolerate it. This is the domain most directly responsible for adoption actually happening — a technically sound AI rollout can still fail here.

## 2. Defining the Scope for AI
The activities that make sure AI adds value to the stakeholders the initiative is actually prioritized for — not scope in the generic project-management sense, but scope specifically disciplined by "does this serve who we said it would serve."

## 3. Designing AI Architecture With Quality and Reliability
Data quality, model development, system architecture, and trust mechanisms. This is the technical backbone domain — where Data Quality (principle 8) becomes concrete practice.

## 4. Executing Strategic AI Goals
Coordinated execution across portfolio, program, and project levels: aligning AI initiatives with business strategy, prioritizing by impact, allocating resources, and running a governance framework that keeps execution ethical and responsible as it happens — not just at design time.

## 5. Managing AI Risks and Uncertainties
Identifying, assessing, and managing the risks introduced by AI — the operational counterpart to the Risk principle. This domain is what turns "we're aware AI has risks" into an actual practice of protecting the organization while still capturing AI's upside.

## How the domains interact
The standard is explicit that these aren't independent checkboxes — they're interdependent and run concurrently throughout an initiative. A useful worked example from the standard: a board decides to prioritize AI in manufacturing → that becomes a portfolio strategy → programs bundle and coordinate the relevant projects → projects deliver. All five domains are active at every one of those levels simultaneously, not handed off in sequence.

Practical uses of this file:
- When scoping a new AI initiative, check that all five domains have an owner and an activity — not just "the technical work" (domain 3) and "the risk review" (domain 5), which are the two people default to.
- When someone says an AI rollout "failed," the domain framing helps diagnose *where*: usually it's domain 1 (stakeholders were told, not engaged) or domain 4 (no real governance framework), even when the failure gets blamed on the technology.

# Healthcare Trade Media Planning

Start healthcare trade publication research and media planning faster by mapping your company's business groups, products, audiences, and regions to the right trade publishers, associations, media kits, rates, and contacts. Covers Dental, Health Information Systems, Medical Surgical, and Biopharma / Life Sciences, with an optional "your accounts" quick-start layer you fill in with your own proven partners, verified contacts, real spend, and decision rationale for accounts you actively manage.

Default deliverable: direct digital publication buys (web banners, newsletter sponsorships, sponsored content, webinar/CE sponsorships, audience extension, lead gen, trade packages). Use print only when the brief asks for it or it comes bundled with a digital package.

## Before you use this skill

This is a generic template, not tied to any one company. It ships with real, publicly sourced trade-publication data (media kits, advertise pages, association contacts) so the routing tables are useful out of the box — but the account-specific layer is intentionally blank.

To make it yours:

1. Search this skill for bracketed placeholders like `[YOUR COMPANY]`, `[YOUR PRODUCT]`, and `[YOUR AGENCY FEE]` and replace them with your own details.
2. Update the business-group names and product-type rows in `references/business-group-routing.md` if your company's structure differs from the Dental / Health IT / Medical Surgical / Biopharma groupings used here — those are just one common way to organize a healthcare trade media practice, not a requirement.
3. Fill in `references/your-accounts.md` over time as you build real publisher relationships, so future requests for the same account skip cold research (see that file for instructions).
4. Re-verify every media kit, rate, and contact in `references/business-group-routing.md`, `references/regional-guide.md`, and `references/sources.md` before relying on them — publisher contacts, rates, and even publications change often, and this data reflects a point-in-time snapshot.

## When to use this skill

Use this whenever the task touches healthcare trade media, including: researching trade publications for a product, building or revising a media plan, shortlisting publishers by audience, drafting publisher/rep outreach, comparing placement options, or quantifying audience reach and rates. It applies even when the user names a specific product or clinical category without saying "trade media."

## First move: is this an account you've worked before?

Before any cold research, check whether the request involves one of your company's active accounts. If so, read `references/your-accounts.md` FIRST — once populated, it holds your proven partners, verified rep contacts, real spend, decision rationale, and creative specs, which makes fresh research unnecessary.

`references/your-accounts.md` ships blank. If it's still empty for the account in question, or the account isn't in it yet, treat this as a new account and go to `references/business-group-routing.md` to build a shortlist from scratch — then consider adding what you learn back into `references/your-accounts.md` for next time.

## Hard rules (always apply)

1. **Verify the current media kit.** Search `[publication] media kit [current year]`, `[publication] advertise`, `[publication] sponsorship`, `[publisher] marketing solutions`. If no current-year kit exists, use the most recent and mark it stale.
2. **Do not assume a contact is current.** Pull contacts from the latest media kit or official advertise page only. Never fabricate a contact.
3. **Separate editorial contacts from ad-sales contacts.** Editorial bylines are not insertion-order contacts unless the kit says so.
4. **Prefer direct publication / association buys first.** Use programmatic audience extension only when the brief allows it.
5. **Regulated-healthcare guardrails.** Require landing-page and creative review. Avoid patient-sensitive targeting, claims implying diagnosis/treatment superiority, or unsubstantiated clinical claims.
6. **Split global by region.** "Global" is not one plan. Build separate shortlists for U.S., Canada, UK/EU, and APAC where possible (see `references/regional-guide.md`).
7. **Tag every recommendation with a fit level:** Primary, Secondary, Test, or Exclude.
8. **Capture minimums and specs only from official kits.** If missing, write "TBD — request from rep."
9. **Verify before agreeing.** Do not confirm a recollection about a placement, swap, rate, or audience until checked against source material (media kit, deck, meeting notes). When uncertain, say so and flag the calibration point rather than projecting false confidence.
10. **Allocate effort and credit by effort drivers, not spend.** When ranking or distributing work across publishers, weight by complexity, first-time vs. repeat relationship, and multi-format build effort.

## Operating conventions (fill in for your company/agency)

These are examples of the kind of standing conventions worth capturing once and reusing — replace them with your own:

- All-in totals = net media + `[YOUR AGENCY FEE %]`. Always show the fee line.
- Note any channel weighting you've learned for a specific brand (e.g., "Brand X is email-weighted — email consistently outperforms other formats").
- Note your house style for client-facing creative copy (e.g., punctuation rules, banned phrasing).
- Present plans in tiers (Plan A/B/C or Option 1/2) with one clear recommendation; dropped options go to an appendix.
- Full account-specific details, timing notes, and lead times belong in `references/your-accounts.md`.

## Workflow

1. Establish business group, product, region, audience, campaign goal, budget, timing. If unknown, infer from the product (see inputs list in `references/research-workflow.md`).
2. Known/active account? → read `references/your-accounts.md`, start from your proven shortlist, re-verify rates with the rep.
3. New/other product? → `references/business-group-routing.md` for the candidate shortlist and product-level recommendations.
4. Non-US or multi-region? → `references/regional-guide.md`.
5. Researching a new publication or building a fresh plan? → `references/research-workflow.md` (inputs to gather, search-query templates, output format, rep-outreach checklist, data-capture schema).
6. Cite media kits and advertise pages from `references/sources.md`; add any newly verified source.
7. Produce the output: a shortlist tagged by fit, a tiered plan with all-in pricing, or outreach drafts (placeholders for unconfirmed rep names).

## Reference files

- `references/your-accounts.md` — blank template for your account-specific quick-start layer: proven partners, verified rep contacts, real spend benchmarks, operating conventions, decision rationale, and creative specs. Fill this in as you build relationships; read it first for any known account.
- `references/business-group-routing.md` — routing tables for Dental, Health Information Systems, Medical Surgical, and Biopharma/Life Sciences: audience splits, publication targets with fit and contact path, shortlist logic, and generic product-type starting points. Swap the example product types for your own product lines.
- `references/regional-guide.md` — strongest publication ecosystems by region (US, Canada, UK/Europe, APAC/Global).
- `references/research-workflow.md` — for building from scratch: inputs to ask for, search-query templates, the standard research output format, the rep-outreach checklist, and the data-capture schema for a publication database.
- `references/sources.md` — media kit and advertise-page URLs for every publisher referenced in this skill, grouped for citation.
